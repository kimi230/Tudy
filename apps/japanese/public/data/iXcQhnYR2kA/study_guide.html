01   Video Structure Overview

This 14-minute video by TEDx Talks is structured into 9 key sections.

1 Section 0:16 ~ 0:19
The speaker opens with a powerful question: if today were your last day, what would you regret? She dismisses trivial regrets (drinking more, dating more) and proposes the real ones: not spending time with family, not chasing dreams, not living honestly.
2 Section 0:16 ~ 0:19
The speaker reveals she once worked as a nurse. She cared for a terminal cancer patient — a grandfather who cried every day, regretting that he spent his whole life working for money instead of living for happiness and family. His final words to her: 'walk a life without regrets'.
3 Section 0:16 ~ 0:19
Moved by the grandfather's words, the speaker begins researching happiness. She discovers on a TV program that Japan, the world's third-largest economy, ranks only 90th in global happiness. She is baffled — shouldn't money bring happiness? — and decides to travel the world to find out.
4 Section 0:16 ~ 0:19
The speaker's first destination is Cambodia's Rusai village — a poor community of about 1,000 people with no electricity, gas, or running water. Yet the children's eyes sparkle and everyone smiles the most beautiful smiles. A villager even sings a song for her, moving her to tears. She realizes: happiness cannot be measured by money or possessions. A Cambodian friend named Sam later visits Japan and confirms: Japan has things but no place to heal the heart.
5 Section 0:16 ~ 0:19
Back in Japan, the speaker is bombarded by society's happiness formula: graduate from a famous university, get a job at a big company, marry a good person, build a house, have children. Each time she hears these messages she feels something is wrong, but goes along with them to avoid standing out.
6 Section 0:16 ~ 0:19
At a group mixer, she meets an unemployed man who wants to travel the world. Rather than being put off, she is intrigued — he lives by his own convictions, outside society's happiness formula. She starts dating him, and they decide to travel the world together.
7 Section 0:16 ~ 0:19
Now married, the couple has traveled 70+ countries across 2 full world trips, including the Uyuni Salt Flats, Machu Picchu, and Ukraine's Tunnel of Love. Through these travels, the speaker's key realization: happiness looks completely different for every person — for some it's family time, for others it's meaningful work or travel.
8 Section 0:16 ~ 0:19
The speaker's answer to why Japanese people are unhappy: they have received too many messages from society about what happiness looks like and have lost sight of their own. She urges the audience to step away from those messages, face themselves honestly, and intentionally create time for self-reflection — away from smartphones and SNS.
9 Section 0:16 ~ 0:19
The speaker closes with a call to action: once you find what happiness means to you, take the first step toward it. Life is shorter than you think. A life without regrets must begin right now, this moment. She ends by asking the audience: what is happiness to you?

04   Grammar Patterns

Key grammar structures used in the video. Each pattern includes real examples.

〜たとしたら (Hypothetical Conditional)
~라고 가정한다면, ~이라면이라는 가정 조건을 나타냅니다. 과거형(た형) + としたら로 구성되며, 있을 법하지 않은 가정적 상황을 상상할 때 사용합니다.
〜そうです (Hearsay / Reported Information)
동사/형용사의 보통형에 붙어 타인에게 들은 정보를 전달합니다: '~라고 합니다', '~라고 들었습니다'. 외관을 나타내는 そうです와 구별됩니다.
〜ことになりました (It has been decided that ~)
상황이 그렇게 되었거나 결정되었음을 나타냅니다. 화자 자신의 의지보다는 외부 상황이나 타인에 의해 그렇게 되었다는 뉘앙스가 있습니다.
おっしゃる (Special Honorific Verb for 言う)
言う(말하다)의 특별 존경어 형태. 윗사람이나 공손하게 대해야 하는 사람이 말씀하실 때 사용합니다.
〜ながら (While doing ~ / Simultaneous Actions)
두 가지 동작이 동시에 이루어짐을 나타냅니다: 'A하면서 B하다'. 주어가 두 행동을 동시에 합니다. 주요 행동이나 감정은 두 번째 절에 옵니다.
〜ながら逆接 (〜のに / Unexpected Contrast with ながら)
ながら는 역접(반전)도 나타낼 수 있습니다: 'A이면서 B'. A라는 조건을 인정하면서 놀랍거나 모순된 결과 B를 보여줍니다.
〜ないと〜ない / 〜なければ〜ない (Negative Conditional: must / cannot unless)
A가 이루어지지 않으면 B도 이루어질 수 없다는 조건 구조입니다. 이 강연에서는 사회적 압박을 열거하는 데 사용됩니다: '~하지 않으면 행복해질 수 없어'.
〜のだろうか (Rhetorical Wonder / Puzzlement)
화자의 의문이나 궁금증을 표현합니다: '~이지 않을까?', '~인 걸까?'. 내성적이고 질문하는 어조를 더합니다.
〜てみる (Try doing ~)
동사의 て형에 붙어 시험삼아 해보는 것을 나타냅니다: '~해 보다'. 어떻게 될지 보기 위해 시험적으로 하는 행동임을 시사합니다.
〜じゃないかと思う (I think it might be ~)
완곡한 주장 패턴: '~이 아닐까 생각합니다', '아마 ~인 것 같습니다'. 단순한 진술보다 부드럽고, 가능한 원인이나 설명을 제안할 때 사용합니다.
〜てみてください (Please try ~)
て형 + みる + ください를 결합한 공손한 요청 표현으로 '~해 보세요'를 의미합니다. 직접적인 명령보다 부드럽고 권유적입니다.
〜なければなりません (Must / Have to ~)
의무를 나타냅니다: '~해야 합니다', '~하지 않으면 안 됩니다'. 부정 조건형 なければ + なりません으로 구성되며 강한 필요성을 나타냅니다.
